    1. Both Local Validity and Global Effectiveness Matter: Decoupled Credit Assignment for Long-Horizon Agentic Learning
    @misc{
lu2026both,
title={Both Local Validity and Global Effectiveness Matter:  Decoupled Credit Assignment for Long\nobreakdash-Horizon Agentic Learning},
author={wenxuan lu and Zhanqiu Zhang and Steven Y. Guo and Tianning Zang},
year={2026},
url={https://openreview.net/forum?id=Suja4AQ9Fo}

    The credit assignment in long CoT has been an issue. In standard episodic GRPO, the final reward is distributed evenly to every token/step, which may mistakenly reward the wrong steps in a trajectory that gives correct final outcome. Some previous work propose "additive" reward assigning paradigm, adding a global reward on top of step reward. However, this still allows some low quality steps to get reward by simply appearing in a good trajectory.

    The author of paper #1 uses a local reward which is approximately ${[-1-\varepsilon,-1+\varepsilon], [1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon]}$, where 1/-1 is decided by the validity of that step. The ultimate reward for the step is $R_\text{step}*R_\text{global}$ so that the always-positive global reward will be turned in to a negative number for the wrong steps, preventing credit misalignment.

    The weaknesses of this work are as follows.

    1. The local reward is highly dependent on environment-specific feedback (API rejections, parsing errors, etc.), limiting generalizability.
    2. The innovation is primarily an empirical heuristic (multiplicative vs. additive rewards) rather than a fundamental algorithmic or theoretical breakthrough.

    3. The heuristic is very naive. If two actions are wrong, one of them has a higher local reward, then the one with higher local reward will be punished harder, which doesn't have a concrete theoretical base.

    4. Why this work cannot be ported directly to math reasoning:

    ​ In the scenario of this work, intermediate reward signal is easy to obtain. In math, intermediate results are hard to validate, while PRM and LLM judge could also provide noisy signals. The core problem of credit assignment in math tasks is still validation and evaluating the quality of intermediate steps.

    • Why this work may not be fundamentally different from additive reward:

    RL learns from advantage function instead of reward function, so as long as the mis-assignment is systematic and predictable, the baseline will adjust accordingly, and the real good steps will still get better rewards compared to the bad ones. Compared to additive reward, this method amplifies the reward difference between a good step and a bad one, which potentially helps to improve the SNR in the training signal, stabilizing the training process.
